Quick start

Choose the next right thing

On a training day, open the workout assigned for today. On other days, take an easy walk if it feels good, eat regular meals, and let recovery happen.

Remember: You can modify, shorten, or skip a movement. A lighter session still counts. Pain is information, not a challenge to push through.

Training plan

30 minutes · 3 days/week
Start with two working sets and a comfortable load. Finish each set feeling you could do another 3–4 good reps. Rest enough to keep technique steady. The time target is flexible.

Four-week progression

Week 1: Learn the movements; keep every set easy.

Week 2: Repeat. Look for smoother reps and less uncertainty.

Week 3: If soreness is mild and resolving, add a rep or a small amount of weight—not both everywhere.

Week 4: Consolidate. Progress only when technique and recovery support it.

If you are still very sore by the next session, repeat or reduce the workload instead of progressing.

Fuel without overthinking

The balanced-meal shortcut

At most meals, aim to include:

ProteinFiber-rich produceCarbohydrateHealthy fat

No single food needs to be perfect. A repeatable pattern matters more than a perfect plate.

Before
1–3 hours before, if usefulEat a normal meal or a snack if hungry or if it has been several hours since you last ate. Examples: banana and yogurt; toast and eggs; fruit and cottage cheese.
After
Within the next few hoursHave your normal meal with protein and carbohydrate. A separate recovery shake or snack is not mandatory if you have eaten and will eat soon.
Daily
Eat enough, consistentlyExamples of carbohydrates include oats, potatoes, beans, fruit, rice, and whole grains. Pairing carbs with protein and fiber can help moderate the post-meal glucose response.

Easy meal ideas

Breakfast: Greek yogurt, oats, berries, and nuts.

Lunch: Chicken, tofu, or beans with rice and vegetables.

Snack: Apple and peanut butter, or cottage cheese and fruit.

Dinner: Salmon, eggs, or lentils with potatoes and vegetables.

Important: Suspected PCOS or insulin resistance needs medical evaluation; this app does not diagnose or treat either. If you take glucose-lowering medication or experience shakiness, sweating, dizziness, or unusual weakness around exercise, get individualized advice about fueling and exercise safety.

What soreness can tell you

Some muscle soreness can happen when returning to exercise, often peaking 24–72 hours later and then improving. It is not required for progress.

For persistent or disproportionate soreness, reduce training volume and review sleep, food intake, and recovery. Sharp or worsening pain, significant weakness, swelling, or dark urine needs prompt medical attention.
